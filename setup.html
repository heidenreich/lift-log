Setup guide · about 10 minutes

Lift Log on your iPhone

Lift Log turns your Strong workouts into a simple picture of your progress: which machines you're getting stronger on, when you're ready for more weight, and whether you're hitting your weekly goals. Your data stays on your phone and is never uploaded.

Step 1

Put it on your home screen

  1. Open this link in Safari. Other browsers can't add it to the home screen.
    Open Lift Logheidenreich.github.io/lift-log
  2. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow pointing up) at the bottom of the screen.
  3. Scroll down, tap Add to Home Screen, then tap Add.
  4. From now on, open Lift Log from the new icon on your home screen.
Step 2

Load your workouts from Strong

  1. Open Strong, go to the Profile tab and tap the gear icon to open Settings.
  2. Tap Export Strong Data, then choose Save to Files and tap Save.
  3. Open Lift Log from your home screen and tap Load Strong CSV.
  4. Pick the file you just saved. It's usually in Recents or On My iPhone.
  5. Tap lb or kg at the top to match the unit you use in Strong.

The home-screen app keeps its own copy of your data, separate from Safari. If you loaded a file in Safari first, load it again from the home-screen icon.

Step 3

Read your progress

The headline
How many exercises you're stronger on since you started, and your biggest gains.
Total weight lifted
Every pound you've lifted added up (weight × reps), how much each workout moves now compared with when you started, and your next milestone. Tap Workout, Week or Month to change the chart.
Weekly goals
The health guidelines for adults: strength training 2 days a week and 150 minutes of moderate activity. A green check means you hit it that week.
Ready to go up
Machines where you've finished every set at the same weight 3 times in a row, with a weight to try next time. Go up when the last reps feel steady, and go back down if your form slips.
Worth a look
Anything more than 10% below your best. That's fine if you lowered it on purpose.
Your exercises
One card per machine: where you started, where you are now, and a small trend line. Tap a card for its full chart.
Body areas
How many days you worked legs, push, pull and core in the last 4 weeks.
Every week or two

Keep it up to date

Lift Log doesn't update on its own. After a few new workouts, repeat Step 2: export from Strong again and load the new file. It replaces the old one, and your history is all there.

Optional

Add your steps and walks

Lift Log can also show daily steps, walking distance and walks from Apple Health. Your walks then count toward the 150-minute weekly goal. A web page can't read Apple Health directly, so a Shortcut saves the numbers to a file you load. You build the first one once, then copy it for the other two.

A. Daily steps

  1. Open the Shortcuts app, tap +, and name the shortcut Lift Log Steps.
  2. Tap Add Action, search for Find Health Samples and add it. Set the type to Steps, add the filter Start Date is in the last 90 days, set Group By to Day, and sort by Start Date, Oldest First.
  3. Add Repeat with Each.
  4. Inside the repeat, add Format Date. Tap the date, choose Repeat Item, then Start Date. Set the format to Custom and type yyyy-MM-dd.
  5. Still inside the repeat, add Text. Put in Formatted Date, type a comma, then add Repeat Item and choose Value.
  6. Below End Repeat, add Combine Text. Set it to combine Repeat Results with New Lines.
  7. Add another Text. Type Date,Steps, start a new line, then add Combined Text.
  8. Add Save File. Turn off Ask Where to Save, set the subpath to lift-log-steps.csv, and turn on Overwrite If File Exists.
  9. Tap ▶ to run it. Allow access to Steps when asked.

B. Walking distance

  1. In your list of shortcuts, press and hold Lift Log Steps, tap Duplicate, and rename the copy Lift Log Distance.
  2. Open it. In Find Health Samples, change the type to Walking + Running Distance.
  3. Change the text Date,Steps to Date,Distance.
  4. In Save File, change the subpath to lift-log-distance.csv.
  5. Run it and allow access when asked.

C. Walks

  1. Duplicate Lift Log Steps again and rename the copy Lift Log Walks.
  2. In Find Health Samples, change the type to Workouts and set Group By to None.
  3. In Format Date, change the custom format to yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm.
  4. Change the Text inside the repeat to: Formatted Date, comma, Repeat Item › Type, comma, Repeat Item › Duration, comma, Repeat Item › Distance.
  5. Change the text Date,Steps to Date,Type,Duration,Distance.
  6. In Save File, change the subpath to lift-log-walks.csv. Run it and allow access to Workouts.

Load them

  1. In Lift Log, tap Load Health files.
  2. Go to Browse › iCloud Drive › Shortcuts, tap Select, pick all three lift-log- files and tap Open.

To refresh these, run the three shortcuts and load the files again. New days are added to what's already saved. You can also have them run on their own: in Shortcuts, open the Automation tab and set each one to run at a time you pick, like Sunday evening.

Good to know

Questions

Who can see my numbers?
Only you. Everything stays in Lift Log on your phone. It isn't sent anywhere, and it isn't shared with anyone else who uses Lift Log.
A menu name doesn't match these steps.
Apple and Strong sometimes rename things. Pick the closest match. The Walks shortcut is the most likely to differ.
I see example numbers instead of mine.
A "Sample" label means your file isn't loaded in this copy of the app. Load it again from the home-screen icon.
A file won't load.
The page shows what went wrong at the top. Send the file and the message to whoever set this up for you, so the page can be adjusted.
